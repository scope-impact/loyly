Found only by looking

Every one of these survived a green suite, because none is expressible as a pass or fail against a spec line written in advance. That is exactly what Ford says informative feedback is for.

  • A high-bound field carrying the low bound over
  • A row overflowing by 98 points at phone width
  • A correctness label floating between two blocks
  • A crowd rule stopping halfway across its card
  • Curve counts colliding at the 100 rung
  • An answered progress segment drawn thinner than its neighbours
  • The Continue button sitting under the software keyboard

What would change

Add a sensor to the build loop: screenshots of the real surface at two widths, handed back with no verdict attached and a question rather than an assertion, so the agent supplies the judgment.

Be honest about the limit. The keyboard defect would still be missed, because a screenshot harness has no software keyboard. A sensor closes some of the gap and not all of it.

A third axis, ours

Two of our mechanisms have no cell at all. The fence removes feed-forward context; the holdback hides a check. Both are about custody rather than content: who may see the harness, and who may write it.

The pieces that do not fit the grid

fence.sh No cell. It removes feed-forward context on purpose, so a regeneration cannot read last week's answer.
held-back items Feedback and normative, and deliberately invisible. The grid has no cell for hiding a check from the thing being measured.
score.py, scoreboard.py Feedback, but not to the agent. It feeds the operator, over weeks.
reality audit Informative feedback where a human supplies the judgment, which is the empty cell staffed by a person.
verify.sh Feedback and normative, but not a loop signal: the agent cannot iterate against a gate it does not control.
